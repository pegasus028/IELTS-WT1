HOW TO PRESENT THIS
================================================================
Two people talking the topic through with each other. The whole episode must be
UNDER SIX MINUTES. No welcome, no introductions, no "today we are going to talk
about", no small talk: the first sentence is the first fact below. Speak slowly and
clearly, in short sentences and everyday CEFR B1 words. Explain every IELTS term in
plain words the first time it is used. Say each example sentence slowly enough to be
written down. Use only the examples given here. Never mention notes, a document, a
source, a file, an app or where the information came from. Never speak to a
listener: no "you", no audience, no goodbye. Cover every point below, in order; if
time is short, shorten the chat around a point, never drop the point. Stop after the
last idea. Say numbers slowly and in full (twenty-five percent, not 25 percent), and
when an example describes a chart, describe the chart in words first.

1. THE NUMBERS (about 1 minute 30)
================================================================

The Writing test is sixty minutes and two tasks. Task 1 is a short factual report on
a chart, a table, a diagram of a process, or a map. It is one third of the Writing
score. Task 2, the essay, counts twice as much. So Task 1 gets twenty minutes, not
thirty. Extra time on the chart is time stolen from the task that pays more.

The minimum is one hundred and fifty words. The safe target is one hundred and sixty
to one hundred and ninety. A report under one hundred and fifty words shows too little
language, and its Task Achievement score stops at Band 5 or below.

The task sentence, called the rubric, is always the same: "Summarise the information
by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant."
Three verbs matter: summarise, select, compare. The rubric never says explain, discuss
or give an opinion.

The answer is connected sentences in paragraphs, never notes or bullet points. Take a
pie chart of how one country made its electricity in 2000: coal forty-eight percent,
natural gas twenty-two percent. "Coal forty-eight, gas twenty-two" in a list is not
accepted. "Coal accounted for forty-eight percent of electricity, while natural gas
made up twenty-two percent" is.

The shape is four paragraphs: an introduction, an overview, and two detail
paragraphs. No conclusion. The twenty minutes have a clock too: thirty seconds to
identify the chart, planning finished by minute four, writing finished by minute
seventeen, and the last three minutes for checking.

================================================================
2. THE FOUR CRITERIA AND THE LADDER (about 2 minutes)
================================================================

Four criteria, each worth twenty-five percent. The Task 1 band is the average.

Task Achievement, or TA: are the main features there, with an overview, figures and
comparisons? Coherence and Cohesion, or CC: is the report organised in paragraphs,
with clear links? Lexical Resource, or LR: is the vocabulary precise, with words in
their usual partners? Grammatical Range and Accuracy, or GRA: are the sentences varied
and mostly correct?

The examiner reads each criterion like a ladder. The official rule: "A script must
fully fit the positive features of the descriptor at a particular level." So the
climb starts low and stops at the first band that is not fully met.

For Task Achievement, the rungs are clear. Band 5: "There may be no data to support
the description." Band 6: "A relevant overview is attempted", and details are
"supported using figures/data". Band 7: "a clear overview", with the data
"appropriately categorised", which means grouped. Band 8: key features "skilfully
selected".

That is why one missing piece is so costly. A report with no overview cannot score
above Band 5 for Task Achievement, however good the grammar is. This is a ceiling, a
limit. It is not "minus one band". It is a roof.

The other criteria have their own gates. CC Band 7 wants "reference and
substitution", words like "this figure" or "the latter", not "Firstly, Secondly" in
every sentence. LR Band 7 wants "awareness of style and collocation", not rare words
used wrongly. GRA Band 7 wants "error-free sentences are frequent". Accuracy, not
showing off.

Every teacher comment belongs to one criterion, and the criterion names the fix. "No
overview" is TA. "One long block with no paragraphs" is CC. "The amount of visitors"
is LR; visitors are counted, so it is "the number of visitors". "Rose to ten percent",
when the chart shows a rise of ten points, is GRA.

The official link to the CEFR: Bands 5.5 to 6.5 are B2, and Bands 7 to 8 are C1. A
Band 7 report is C1 writing.

================================================================
3. WHAT THE EXAMINER PUNISHES (about 1 minute 30)
================================================================

Task 1 is a factual report. The official guidance says candidates are not asked to
interpret the data or to give their opinion. So four things lose marks just by being
there.

One: opinion. A bar chart shows how many cups of coffee one person drinks a week in
five countries, with Finland highest at twenty-one cups. "Coffee consumption in
Finland was by far the highest, at twenty-one cups a week, which is surprising."
Everything is right until "which is surprising". That is the writer's feeling, not
the chart. The sentence should stop at "a week". No "I think", no "interesting", no
"good news".

Two: reasons. A bar chart shows weekly screen time for four age groups, and gaming
falls from twelve hours for teenagers to one hour for people over fifty. "Older
people gamed less because they were busier with work." The chart shows no reason, so
this is a guess. An opinion or a reason in a detail sentence holds Task Achievement
at Band 6 or below. In the overview, at Band 5 or below.

Three: a conclusion. "In conclusion, the chart shows many interesting changes" adds
nothing. The report simply ends with its last detail sentence.

Four: copied words. The descriptors say "Any copied rubric must be discounted." Words
copied from the task sentence are taken out of the word count and show nothing about
the writer's English.

And one more fault costs marks by being missing: figures. A report full of claims and
empty of numbers is the Band 5 picture of "no data to support the description".

================================================================
THE THING WORTH REMEMBERING
================================================================

Twenty minutes, one hundred and sixty to one hundred and ninety words, four
paragraphs, and only what the chart shows. The examiner climbs a ladder, and one
missing rung, like the overview, stops the climb.

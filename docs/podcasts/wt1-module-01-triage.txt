QUILLMOOR OBSERVATORY — MODULE 01 PODCAST BRIEFING
IELTS ACADEMIC WRITING TASK 1 · MODULE 01: TRIAGE
Listeners: Thai high school students, CEFR A2 to B2, aiming for Writing Band 7 to 8
Topic: the first thirty seconds — the four modes, reading units and labels, and
choosing the tense from the time frame

================================================================
HOW TO PRESENT THIS
================================================================
Two people talking the topic through with each other. The whole episode must be
UNDER SIX MINUTES. No welcome, no introductions, no "today we are going to talk
about", no small talk: the first sentence is the first fact below. Speak slowly and
clearly, in short sentences and everyday CEFR B1 words. Explain every IELTS term in
plain words the first time it is used. Say each example sentence slowly enough to be
written down. Use only the examples given here. Never mention notes, a document, a
source, a file, an app or where the information came from. Never speak to a
listener: no "you", no audience, no goodbye. Cover every point below, in order; if
time is short, shorten the chat around a point, never drop the point. Stop after the
last idea. Say numbers slowly and in full (twenty-five percent, not 25 percent), and
when an example describes a chart, describe the chart in words first.

================================================================
1. FOUR MODES (about 1 minute 45)
================================================================

Triage is a word from hospitals: a quick first look that decides what happens next.
In Task 1, triage takes thirty seconds, before a single number is read. It answers
four questions.

Is there a time axis, or two or more dates? Then the mode is TREND: things rise,
fall and peak. No dates, only categories such as countries or age groups? COMPARE:
things are higher, lower, the largest. A diagram of stages? SEQUENCE: first, next,
finally. A map or a plan? SPACE: north, south, built, demolished. And the fourth
question: how many visuals are there? Two visuals need one paragraph each.

The mode decides the verbs, the tense and the linking words before the writing
starts.

The shape of the chart does not decide the mode. The time does. A bar chart of
international tourist arrivals in Thailand, with bars for the years 2015 to 2025, is
TREND: arrivals rose, collapsed and recovered. A bar chart of weekly screen time for
four age groups in 2023 is COMPARE. Two pie charts of electricity sources, one for
2000 and one for 2020, are TREND, because there are two dates. A table of rice
production with the years 2000, 2010 and 2020 in its columns is TREND too.

The classic mistake is a trend verb on a COMPARE chart. On the screen-time chart,
gaming is twelve hours a week for teenagers and one hour for people over fifty. "Gaming
increased from the over-fifties to the teenagers" is wrong. Nothing increased; these
are different people in the same year. The correct sentence: "Teenagers spent far more
time gaming than the over-fifties." A trend verb on a chart with no time counts as
inaccurate content.

================================================================
2. UNITS, AXES AND LABELS (about 1 minute 30)
================================================================

Many wrong figures come from misreading the unit, not the number. So triage also reads
the title, both axes, the units and the legend.

A line graph shows visitors to three museums in a European city from 2010 to 2020.
The axis says "thousand visitors". The Science Museum line ends at seven hundred and
sixty. That means seven hundred and sixty thousand visitors, not seven hundred and
sixty. On the tourist bar chart the axis says "millions", so the 2019 bar at
thirty-nine point nine means thirty-nine point nine million arrivals. On a pie chart,
a slice of forty-eight means forty-eight percent, a share of the whole, not forty-eight
tonnes.

Small words in the title change the meaning. "Average hours per week" is one typical
person in one week, not the total for a whole group. "Percentage of households" can
never be more than one hundred. A table can mix units: a table of teachers' pay in
five countries has two columns in US dollars and a third column in hours per week.

So the unit travels with the number in every sentence. A line graph shows the
percentage of households with internet access in four countries. "In 2025,
ninety-seven households in Sweden had internet access" is wrong. "In 2025,
ninety-seven percent of households in Sweden had internet access" is right.

================================================================
3. TIME FRAME AND TENSE (about 1 minute 45)
================================================================

The dates found in triage decide the tense.

All dates in the past: past simple. "Between 2010 and 2020, the Science Museum rose
from four hundred and twenty thousand to seven hundred and sixty thousand visitors."
Rose, not rises. A chart with one past year, like the screen-time chart for 2023,
also takes the past simple: teenagers spent, not spend. The present perfect, "has
risen", fits only when the period runs up to now.

Dates in the future are projections, forecasts that have not happened yet. A line
graph shows the populations of Lagos, Osaka and Bangkok from 1990, with a dotted line
after 2025. The dotted part is a forecast. "Lagos had twenty-four million people in
2040" reports a guess as a fact. The correct sentence: "Lagos is expected to reach
twenty-four million by 2040." Or: "Osaka is projected to fall slightly, to seventeen
point eight million by 2040."

No dates at all: present simple. A process diagram shows how glass bottles are
recycled. This process is man-made, so the passive is natural: "The used
bottles are collected at a bottle bank and then washed." A natural process, like the
life cycle of a frog, uses active verbs: "The tadpole hatches after about ten days."

Maps with two dates mix tenses on purpose. Two maps show the centre of a town called
Riverside in 1995 and 2025. In 1995 there is a factory in the east; in 2025 there are
apartment blocks in the same place. "The factory was demolished and apartment blocks
were built in its place." The past simple passive reports a finished change. A map of
plans for the future takes "will be built".

================================================================
THE THING WORTH REMEMBERING
================================================================

Before reading any number: time or no time, unit, and dates. Those thirty seconds
decide the mode, the unit in every sentence and the tense, and they prevent the
mistakes that look like careless reading.

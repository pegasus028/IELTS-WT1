QUILLMOOR OBSERVATORY — MODULE 14 PODCAST BRIEFING
IELTS ACADEMIC WRITING TASK 1 · MODULE 14: EXAM DAY
Listeners: Thai high school students, CEFR A2 to B2, aiming for Writing Band 7 to 8
Topic: the twenty-minute plan, the ten-point check in the last three minutes, and
what to do on paper, on screen and when time runs out

================================================================
HOW TO PRESENT THIS
================================================================
Two people talking the topic through with each other. The whole episode must be
UNDER SIX MINUTES. No welcome, no introductions, no "today we are going to talk
about", no small talk: the first sentence is the first fact below. Speak slowly and
clearly, in short sentences and everyday CEFR B1 words. Explain every IELTS term in
plain words the first time it is used. Say each example sentence slowly enough to be
written down. Use only the examples given here. Never mention notes, a document, a
source, a file, an app or where the information came from. Never speak to a
listener: no "you", no audience, no goodbye. Cover every point below, in order; if
time is short, shorten the chat around a point, never drop the point. Stop after the
last idea. Say numbers slowly and in full (twenty-five percent, not 25 percent), and
when an example describes a chart, describe the chart in words first.

================================================================
1. THE TWENTY-MINUTE PLAN (about 1 minute 45)
================================================================

The Writing test is sixty minutes for two tasks. Task 2, the essay, counts twice as
much as Task 1, so Task 1 is one third of the Writing score. That is why Task 1 gets
twenty minutes, never thirty. Ten extra minutes on the chart might add a few words;
ten minutes lost on the essay can cost far more.

The writers who do best do not start writing at once. The twenty minutes have five
phases. From zero to thirty seconds: triage, a quick sort. What type of visual is
it, which mode is it, meaning TREND for change over time, COMPARE for categories,
SEQUENCE for a process or SPACE for a map, what are the units, what are the dates,
and how many visuals are there? From thirty seconds to two and a half minutes: the
big picture, with two notes in the margin for the overview. From two and a half
minutes to four minutes: group and select. Name two groups and circle three to five
figures for each.

Take a table of rice production in four countries in 2000, 2010 and 2020, in million
tonnes. China produces the most every year, India rises the most, and Thailand is
the only country to fall after 2010. Four minutes of planning looks like this in the
margin: "TREND. Million tonnes. 2000 to 2020. China largest. India biggest rise.
Thailand down after 2010."

From four minutes to seventeen: write the four paragraphs, about thirty, forty-five,
sixty and sixty words. The overview goes immediately after the introduction, never
at the end. Without an overview, Task Achievement cannot go above Band 5, so it must
be on the page early. From seventeen minutes to twenty: check, then stop. There is
no conclusion.

================================================================
2. THE TEN-POINT CHECK (about 1 minute 45)
================================================================

The last three minutes are for ten quick questions. One: four paragraphs, with the
overview starting "Overall,"? Two: is the overview general, with no data values?
Three: does every detail sentence have a number, date or label? Four: at least two
clear comparisons? Five: does the tense match the dates? Six: "number of" for things
that can be counted, "amount of" for things that cannot, and plural endings in
place? Seven: the right prepositions, to, by, from and at? Eight: no opinion, no
reason, no conclusion, no "I", no contractions? Nine: the task sentence not copied,
and every category mentioned? Ten: between one hundred and fifty and one hundred and
ninety words?

Points one, two and eight are about shape and take seconds. Points three to seven
need one read of each detail sentence.

Take a bar chart of international tourist arrivals in Thailand from 2015 to 2025: a
peak in 2019, a collapse in 2020 and 2021, and a strong recovery after that. This
overview passes point two: "Overall, arrivals rose to a peak in 2019, collapsed in
2020 and 2021, and then recovered strongly." Years are allowed in an overview; data
values are not.

Point eight is the one students forget. Register means the style of the writing, and
a report needs a formal style. "As we can see, the number of tourists dropped a lot
in 2020, which is really sad." That sentence fails three times: "we" is personal, "a
lot" is spoken English, and "really sad" is an opinion. The report version:
"Arrivals fell sharply to six point seven million in 2020." A conclusion found at
point eight is simply crossed out. The overview already does that job.

================================================================
3. PAPER, SCREEN, AND WHEN TIME RUNS OUT (about 1 minute 30)
================================================================

On paper, counting every word takes too long. Instead, the writer counts the words
on one full line, multiplies by the number of full lines, and adds the short lines
at paragraph ends. Eleven words a line times fifteen lines is about one hundred and
sixty-five words. A blank line between paragraphs shows the examiner four clear
blocks.

On the computer test, the screen shows a word count, so it is checked at the end of
each paragraph. A useful habit is to type four short labels first, such as Intro,
Overall, Group A and Group B, fill them in, then delete the labels. Cut and paste
can move a sentence instead of retyping it. There is no spell checker, so the
ten-point check matters just as much on screen.

Task order is a choice, made before the exam, not in the room. A student who often
runs over time on Task 1 should write Task 2 first, because it counts double. A
student who writes Task 1 first needs a hard stop at twenty minutes.

When time runs out, the overview is already in paragraph two, so the report can
still reach its band. The writer finishes the sentence she is writing, then checks
three things: "Overall" is there, each detail sentence has a figure, and the count
is above one hundred and fifty. Under one hundred and fifty words, Task Achievement
cannot go above Band 5. If a whole category is still missing, one sentence naming it
with a figure comes first. If words are short, the best extra sentence is one
comparison with two figures. "In conclusion, rice production changed a lot" earns
nothing. The target stays one hundred and sixty to one hundred and ninety words; far
beyond that, extra sentences add errors, not marks.

================================================================
THE THING WORTH REMEMBERING
================================================================

Four minutes to plan, thirteen to write, three to check, and a hard stop at twenty.
The overview goes second, so the most important paragraph is always on the page, and
the last three minutes belong to the ten-point check, never to a conclusion.

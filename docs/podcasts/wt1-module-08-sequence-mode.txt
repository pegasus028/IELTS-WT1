QUILLMOOR OBSERVATORY — MODULE 08 PODCAST BRIEFING
IELTS ACADEMIC WRITING TASK 1 · MODULE 08: SEQUENCE MODE
Listeners: Thai high school students, CEFR A2 to B2, aiming for Writing Band 7 to 8
Topic: process diagrams: present simple, passive or active voice, sequencers that
link the stages, and the overview of a process

================================================================
HOW TO PRESENT THIS
================================================================
Two people talking the topic through with each other. The whole episode must be
UNDER SIX MINUTES. No welcome, no introductions, no "today we are going to talk
about", no small talk: the first sentence is the first fact below. Speak slowly and
clearly, in short sentences and everyday CEFR B1 words. Explain every IELTS term in
plain words the first time it is used. Say each example sentence slowly enough to be
written down. Use only the examples given here. Never mention notes, a document, a
source, a file, an app or where the information came from. Never speak to a
listener: no "you", no audience, no goodbye. Cover every point below, in order; if
time is short, shorten the chat around a point, never drop the point. Stop after the
last idea. Say numbers slowly and in full (twenty-five percent, not 25 percent), and
when an example describes a chart, describe the chart in words first.

================================================================
1. PRESENT SIMPLE, PASSIVE OR ACTIVE (about 1 minute 30)
================================================================

SEQUENCE mode is for process diagrams: pictures of stages, not years. The first
diagram shows how glass bottles are recycled, in a loop of eight stages. Used bottles
are collected at a bottle bank. They go by lorry to a recycling plant. They are
washed with high-pressure water, then sorted by colour into clear, green and brown.
The glass is crushed into small pieces called cullet. The cullet is melted in a
furnace with new raw materials. The hot liquid glass is shaped into new bottles in a
mould, and the new bottles are filled and delivered to shops.

A process has no dates, so every verb is present simple. The most common error is the
past simple: "The bottles were sorted by colour." The fix: "The bottles are sorted by
colour into clear, green and brown."

For a man-made process, nobody cares who does the work, so the voice is passive: is
or are, plus the past participle. "At the first stage, used bottles are collected at
a bottle bank." Plural subject, "are". "The cullet is melted in a furnace together
with new raw materials." Singular subject, "is".

A natural process is different. The second diagram is the life cycle of a frog, five
stages in a loop. Eggs are laid in water. After about ten days, a tadpole hatches,
with gills and a tail. Back legs grow, then front legs. By about twelve weeks, the
young frog, called a froglet, has lungs and a shrinking tail. The adult frog lives on
land and in water, and lays eggs. Nature does this by itself, so the verbs are
active: "After about ten days, the tadpole hatches." "The tadpole is hatched" sounds
as if someone hatched it.

Diagram labels are usually nouns, like Washing or Furnace. The writer turns each one
into a verb: washed, melted.

================================================================
2. SEQUENCERS (about 2 minutes)
================================================================

A process is a chain, and sequencers are the links: words that move the reader from
one stage to the next. "At the first stage", "Next", "Following this", "After this",
"Finally". One sequencer per stage, and never the same one twice in a row.

The weak version: "Then it is crushed. Then it is melted. Then it is moulded. Then it
is filled." The grammar is correct, but it sounds like a machine. Coherence and
Cohesion, the score for organisation and linking, says at Band 6 that cohesion "may
be faulty or mechanical".

Three sequencers join two stages inside one sentence, and that is the complex grammar
Band 7 wants. First, "once" with "has been" or "have been": "Once the bottles have
been washed, they are sorted by colour into clear, green and brown." Second, "before
being": "The molten glass is moulded into new bottles before being filled and
delivered to shops." Third, "after which", for the last stage of a cycle: "The new
bottles are delivered to shops, after which the cycle begins again."

In a natural cycle, the same tools work with active verbs, and time phrases from the
diagram become sequencers that also carry data: "After about ten days, the eggs hatch
into tadpoles, which then grow back legs, followed by front legs."

Plan them before writing: "At the first stage" for stage one, "Finally" for the last,
and the others in between. A good process report uses at least three different
sequencers.

================================================================
3. THE OVERVIEW OF A PROCESS (about 1 minute 30)
================================================================

A process has no trend, so many students skip the overview, the "Overall" paragraph.
Without an overview, Task Achievement cannot go above Band 5.

A process overview answers four questions. How many stages? Is it linear, a line from
start to end, or cyclical, a loop? What goes in at the start? What comes out at the
end? "Overall, the process is cyclical and consists of eight stages, beginning with
the collection of used bottles and ending with new bottles being delivered to shops."

A second sentence can split the stages into two phases: "The stages fall into two
phases: preparing the waste glass, and making and distributing the new product." That
sentence also plans the report: stages one to four in paragraph three, stages five to
eight in paragraph four.

For the frog: "Overall, the life cycle is natural and has five stages, from eggs laid
in water to an adult frog that lays eggs again."

Count the boxes carefully. A report that misses one stage, say the crushing, has left
out a key feature, and Task Achievement cannot go above Band 6.

The overview has no measurements and no opinion. Here is a line with two faults:
"Overall, the process has four stages and recycling is important for the planet." The
count is wrong, there are eight, and the opinion does not belong in a report.

================================================================
THE THING WORTH REMEMBERING
================================================================

Present simple always; passive for man-made, active for nature. A different sequencer
for every stage, and an overview that gives the shape, the number of stages, the
start and the end.

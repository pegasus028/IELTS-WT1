QUILLMOOR OBSERVATORY — MODULE 05 PODCAST BRIEFING
IELTS ACADEMIC WRITING TASK 1 · MODULE 05: GROUPING
Listeners: Thai high school students, CEFR A2 to B2, aiming for Writing Band 7 to 8
Topic: sorting the data into two groups before writing, one group per detail
paragraph, and choosing which figures each group keeps

================================================================
HOW TO PRESENT THIS
================================================================
Two people talking the topic through with each other. The whole episode must be
UNDER SIX MINUTES. No welcome, no introductions, no "today we are going to talk
about", no small talk: the first sentence is the first fact below. Speak slowly and
clearly, in short sentences and everyday CEFR B1 words. Explain every IELTS term in
plain words the first time it is used. Say each example sentence slowly enough to be
written down. Use only the examples given here. Never mention notes, a document, a
source, a file, an app or where the information came from. Never speak to a
listener: no "you", no audience, no goodbye. Cover every point below, in order; if
time is short, shorten the chat around a point, never drop the point. Stop after the
last idea. Say numbers slowly and in full (twenty-five percent, not 25 percent), and
when an example describes a chart, describe the chart in words first.

================================================================
1. SORT BEFORE WRITING (about 1 minute 30)
================================================================

A chart is not a list. Before writing the two detail paragraphs, paragraphs three and
four, a strong writer looks at every category and asks: which of these belong
together? A category is one line, one bar, one slice, one row of a table or one
building on a map. The aim is two groups. Each group becomes one paragraph.

The usual answers are rising versus falling, large versus small, changed a lot versus
stayed the same, and, on a map, north versus south.

The chart: a line graph of visitors to three museums in a European city from 2010 to
2020. The Science Museum rose from 420,000 to 760,000. The Art Gallery fell from
610,000 to 520,000, and the History Museum fell from 380,000 to 260,000. So Group A
is the museum that rose. Group B is the two museums that fell.

Every group needs a name. "The Science Museum" is not a group name; "the museum that
grew" is.

A second chart: two pie charts showing where one country's electricity came from in
2000 and 2020. Coal fell from 48 to 18 percent, wind and solar rose from 5 to 27
percent, and natural gas rose from 22 to 30 percent. Nuclear went from 15 to 14
percent, and hydro from 10 to 11. So the groups are "changed a lot" and "hardly
changed".

Grouping pays twice. Task Achievement, the score for how well the report covers the
chart, asks at Band 7 that "the data are appropriately categorised". Coherence and
Cohesion, the score for organisation, asks at Band 7 that "information and ideas are
logically organised". It takes about sixty seconds of planning. Without it, a student
writes one paragraph per museum, and the Band 5 descriptor calls that kind of report
"mainly mechanical".

================================================================
2. ONE GROUP, ONE PARAGRAPH (about 2 minutes)
================================================================

Paragraph three is Group A. Paragraph four is Group B. Paragraph three opens by
saying what the group is and what it did, then gives two or three sentences with
figures. Paragraph four opens with a contrast word: "In contrast", "By comparison" or
"On the other hand".

Inside the paragraph, the order is natural. For a group that moves over time, use
time order: the start, the middle event, then the end. A middle event is a peak, or a
crossover, the moment one line goes past another. For a group with no time, use size
order: the biggest first.

Here is paragraph three for the museums: "In 2010, the Science Museum attracted
420,000 visitors. Its numbers then climbed steadily, overtaking the Art Gallery by
2014, and reached 760,000 in 2020." Start, crossover, end.

And paragraph four: "In contrast, the other two museums both lost visitors. Visitors
to the Art Gallery fell from 610,000 to 520,000, while the History Museum, the least
visited throughout, ended at 260,000." Contrast opener, group name, figures, a
comparison.

The test for every sentence: does it belong to this group? Imagine a paragraph about
the energy sources that changed a lot: "The share of coal fell from 48% to 18%. Hydro
stayed at about 10%. Wind and solar rose to 27%." The hydro sentence is in the wrong
paragraph. It belongs with nuclear, in the "hardly changed" group. A sentence that
mentions two museums goes where its subject goes: the crossover sentence is about the
Science Museum, so it stays in paragraph three.

================================================================
3. LEAVE THINGS OUT (about 1 minute 30)
================================================================

Choosing figures is the second half of grouping. The museum graph has three lines and
six years: eighteen numbers. A strong report uses about eight of them. Band 8 Task
Achievement wants key features that are "skilfully selected". Leaving numbers out is
not lazy. It is the skill being tested.

The figures that stay are predictable. For a group that changes over time: the first
value, the last value, the extreme, meaning the peak or the lowest point, and one
comparison with the other group. For a group with no time: the largest, the smallest,
and one gap between them. A middle value only stays if something happens there, like
a crossover or a sudden collapse.

Here is the list that loses marks: "The Art Gallery had 610,000 visitors in 2010,
590,000 in 2012, 560,000 in 2014, 570,000 in 2016, 540,000 in 2018 and 520,000 in
2020." Six figures, no choice. Keep 610,000 and 520,000.

A second chart: a pie chart of how a secondary school student in one city spent her
weekly money in 2024. Food and drinks was 38 percent, transport 22, phone and
internet 15, clothes 12, entertainment 9 and savings 4. Keep the largest, the second
largest and the smallest. The three middle slices go together in one phrase: "Phone
and internet, clothes and entertainment each made up between 9% and 15%."

================================================================
THE THING WORTH REMEMBERING
================================================================

Two named groups, one paragraph each, and three to five figures per group. Every
other number stays out on purpose.

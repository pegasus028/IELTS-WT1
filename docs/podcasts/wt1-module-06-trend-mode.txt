QUILLMOOR OBSERVATORY — MODULE 06 PODCAST BRIEFING
IELTS ACADEMIC WRITING TASK 1 · MODULE 06: TREND MODE
Listeners: Thai high school students, CEFR A2 to B2, aiming for Writing Band 7 to 8
Topic: the verbs and nouns of change, words for size and speed, and peaks, low points
and projections on any chart with a time axis

================================================================
HOW TO PRESENT THIS
================================================================
Two people talking the topic through with each other. The whole episode must be
UNDER SIX MINUTES. No welcome, no introductions, no "today we are going to talk
about", no small talk: the first sentence is the first fact below. Speak slowly and
clearly, in short sentences and everyday CEFR B1 words. Explain every IELTS term in
plain words the first time it is used. Say each example sentence slowly enough to be
written down. Use only the examples given here. Never mention notes, a document, a
source, a file, an app or where the information came from. Never speak to a
listener: no "you", no audience, no goodbye. Cover every point below, in order; if
time is short, shorten the chat around a point, never drop the point. Stop after the
last idea. Say numbers slowly and in full (twenty-five percent, not 25 percent), and
when an example describes a chart, describe the chart in words first.

================================================================
1. VERBS AND NOUNS OF CHANGE (about 1 minute 30)
================================================================

TREND mode is for any chart with a time axis, or with two or more dates. Its language
is a small, closed kit. Up: rose, increased, grew, climbed. Down: fell, decreased,
dropped, declined. Flat: remained stable, levelled off. Up and down again and again:
fluctuated. Ten verbs used correctly score higher than thirty verbs used badly.

Each verb has a noun partner: rose and a rise, fell and a fall, dropped and a drop,
declined and a decline. That gives two sentence patterns. The chart: a line graph of
visitors to three museums in a European city from 2010 to 2020. The Science Museum
went up from 420,000 to 760,000. Pattern S3 uses the verb: "Visitor numbers at the
Science Museum rose from 420,000 in 2010 to 760,000 in 2020." Pattern S4 uses the
noun: "There was a steady rise in visitor numbers at the Science Museum, from 420,000
to 760,000." Using both is what examiners call range, meaning variety.

The dates are in the past, so the verbs are past simple. Watch the irregular ones:
rise becomes rose, never "rised"; fall becomes fell; grow becomes grew.

The verb must be true of the whole line. The Art Gallery went 610, 590, 560, 570,
540, then 520 thousand. One small bump in 2016 does not make it fluctuate. Overall,
it fell.

Strength matters too. A bar chart shows international tourist arrivals in Thailand:
39.9 million in 2019, then just 0.4 million in 2021. "Declined" is true but far too
weak. This is a collapse, so the verb is "plummeted".

================================================================
2. HOW BIG AND HOW FAST (about 1 minute 30)
================================================================

A bare verb gives the direction. A degree word gives the size. After a verb, use an
adverb: "rose sharply". Before a noun, use an adjective: "a sharp rise". Learn them
in pairs: sharp and sharply, dramatic and dramatically, significant and
significantly, slight and slightly, marginal and marginally. "Rose sharp" and "a
sharply rise" are both errors.

Some words describe speed instead of size: gradually, steadily, rapidly.

The degree word must match the number. The chart: two pie charts of one country's
electricity sources in 2000 and 2020. Coal fell from 48 to 18 percent. That is huge:
"The share of coal fell dramatically from 48% in 2000 to 18% in 2020." Nuclear went
from 15 to 14 percent. That is tiny: "There was a marginal decline in the share of
nuclear power, from 15% to 14%."

Another chart: a line graph of households with internet access in four countries from
2005 to 2025. Thailand went from 12 to 88 percent: "Internet access in Thailand rose
rapidly, from 12% to 88% over the twenty years."

Now the trap. The Art Gallery lost 90,000 of its 610,000 visitors, about one in
seven. "Visitors to the Art Gallery fell dramatically" is wrong. "Fell gradually"
fits. Lexical Resource, the vocabulary score, asks at Band 7 for "some flexibility
and precision". Precision means the word fits the number. A big word on a small
change is an accuracy error, not good vocabulary.

================================================================
3. PEAKS, LOW POINTS AND THE FUTURE (about 2 minutes)
================================================================

The highest and lowest points on a line are almost always key features. The highest
point: "peaked at". The lowest: "hit a low of". The small words matter: "peaked at",
but "a low of" and "a high of".

Back to the Thai tourism bar chart. Arrivals climbed from 29.9 million in 2015 to
39.9 million in 2019, crashed to 0.4 million in 2021, then recovered to 36.5 million
in 2025. Join the extreme to what came next: "Arrivals peaked at 39.9 million in 2019
before collapsing to just 0.4 million in 2021." And: "The figure hit a low of 0.4
million in 2021 and then recovered to 36.5 million by 2025." Two facts in one
sentence. That is the kind of complex sentence Band 7 grammar asks for: "a variety of
complex structures".

Some graphs continue into the future with a dotted line. Everything after the last
real year is a projection, a forecast. The tense changes: "is expected to", "is
projected to", or "will reach".

The chart: a line graph of the populations of three cities from 1990 to 2025, with
projections to 2040. Lagos grew from 4.8 million to 16.5 million by 2025 and is
forecast to reach 24 million by 2040. Osaka peaked at 19.3 million in 2010 and is
forecast to fall to 17.8 million by 2040. So: "Lagos rose to 16.5 million in 2025 and
is projected to reach 24 million by 2040." And: "Osaka peaked at 19.3 million in 2010
and is expected to decline to 17.8 million by 2040." One sentence, two time frames,
two verb forms, each verb matching its year.

The error: "Lagos reached 24 million in 2040." That year has not happened. Past
simple for a future year is a tense error the examiner sees at once.

================================================================
THE THING WORTH REMEMBERING
================================================================

Choose the verb from the whole line, the degree word from the size of the change, and
the tense from the year. A dotted line means "is projected to", never the past.

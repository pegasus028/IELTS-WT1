QUILLMOOR OBSERVATORY — MODULE 07 PODCAST BRIEFING
IELTS ACADEMIC WRITING TASK 1 · MODULE 07: COMPARE MODE
Listeners: Thai high school students, CEFR A2 to B2, aiming for Writing Band 7 to 8
Topic: comparing categories when nothing changes over time: bar charts without years,
single pie charts, and tables

================================================================
HOW TO PRESENT THIS
================================================================
Two people talking the topic through with each other. The whole episode must be
UNDER SIX MINUTES. No welcome, no introductions, no "today we are going to talk
about", no small talk: the first sentence is the first fact below. Speak slowly and
clearly, in short sentences and everyday CEFR B1 words. Explain every IELTS term in
plain words the first time it is used. Say each example sentence slowly enough to be
written down. Use only the examples given here. Never mention notes, a document, a
source, a file, an app or where the information came from. Never speak to a
listener: no "you", no audience, no goodbye. Cover every point below, in order; if
time is short, shorten the chat around a point, never drop the point. Stop after the
last idea. Say numbers slowly and in full (twenty-five percent, not 25 percent), and
when an example describes a chart, describe the chart in words first.

================================================================
1. BAR CHARTS: HIGHER, HIGHEST, TWICE AS (about 2 minutes)
================================================================

COMPARE mode is for charts with no time axis: only categories, such as age groups,
subjects or countries. When nothing moves through time, nothing rises or falls.

The chart: a bar chart of the hours per week that people in four age groups in one
country spent on three activities in 2023. For social media, the figures were 21
hours for ages 13 to 17, then 18, 11, and 6 hours for people aged 50 and over.
Streaming video was between 12 and 15 hours in every group. Gaming ranged from 12
hours for teenagers to just 1 hour for the over-50s.

The classic mistake: "Gaming fell sharply with age." "Fell" is a trend verb, and this
chart has no time. The fix: "Gaming was far lower among older people."

Three tools do most of the work. Comparatives compare two bars: higher than, lower
than. Superlatives name the extreme: the highest, the lowest. Multiples give the size
of the gap: twice as much as, four times as much as.

Pattern S5 is the comparative with both figures: "Social media use was far higher
among people aged 13 to 17 than among those aged 50 and over, at 21 and 6 hours a
week respectively." "Respectively" means "in the same order". The figures follow the
order of the names, and the word goes at the end. Two names, two figures; never more
than three.

Pattern S6 is the multiple: "Teenagers spent four times as much time gaming as people
aged 30 to 49." That is 12 hours against 3. The arithmetic must be right, or the
sentence becomes an accuracy error.

Near-equal bars get their own phrase: "Streaming was roughly equal across the four
age groups, at between 12 and 15 hours." This chart is dated 2023, so the verbs are
past simple: "was", "spent". And after a number above one, the noun is plural: five
hours.

================================================================
2. PIE CHARTS: SHARES AND FRACTIONS (about 1 minute 30)
================================================================

A single pie chart shows shares of a whole: every slice is part of one hundred
percent. The verbs are proportion verbs: accounted for, made up, represented.

The chart: a pie chart of how a secondary school student in one city spent her weekly
money in 2024. Food and drinks was 38 percent, transport 22, phone and internet 15,
clothes 12, entertainment 9, and savings only 4.

The error: "Savings rose to 4%." Nothing rose. It is one pie, one year. The fix:
"Savings represented just 4%."

Fractions add range. 22 percent is just under a quarter. 38 percent is well over a
third. 48 percent is almost half. Always pair the fraction with the exact figure:
"Food and drinks accounted for the largest share, at 38%, well over a third of the
total." The fraction shows vocabulary; the figure keeps the sentence accurate.

Two moves fit in one sentence. Combine the big slices, then sweep up the small ones:
"Together, food and transport made up 60% of the weekly budget, while the remaining
four categories shared the other 40%."

One warning: two pie charts for two different years are not pure COMPARE mode. The
change between the years takes trend verbs: "the share of coal fell from 48% to 18%".

================================================================
3. TABLES: WHOLE ROWS AND COLUMNS (about 1 minute 30)
================================================================

A table is a bar chart without the bars. The danger is that every number looks
equally important. It is not.

The chart: a table of secondary school teachers in five countries in 2022, with three
columns: starting salary, salary after 15 years, and hours worked per week. Germany:
62,000 dollars, 81,000 dollars, 40 hours. The USA: 44,000, 66,000, 46 hours. Korea:
33,000, 58,000, 43 hours. Japan: 31,000, 55,000, 54 hours. Thailand: 9,000, 17,000,
45 hours.

Read the table twice: down each column, then along each row. Find the biggest and the
smallest number in each. Those are the key features.

Then compare whole rows or whole columns, not single cells. A whole row: "German
teachers were the best paid at both stages, at $62,000 and $81,000, and worked the
shortest week, at 40 hours." A whole column: "Starting salaries ranged from just
$9,000 in Thailand to $62,000 in Germany, almost seven times as much." Two columns of
one row, in contrast: "Japanese teachers worked the longest hours, at 54 a week,
whereas their starting salary was among the lowest, at $31,000."

Now the list that loses marks: "Korea's starting salary was $33,000. Korea's salary
after 15 years was $58,000. Korean teachers worked 43 hours a week." Three cells, and
nothing is compared.

Check the mode first. If the columns are years, for example rice production in 2000,
2010 and 2020, the table is TREND and needs rose and fell. If the columns are
measures, like salary and hours, it is COMPARE.

================================================================
THE THING WORTH REMEMBERING
================================================================

No time axis means no rising and no falling. Rank the categories, give the gap as a
multiple or a fraction, and always compare two things in the same sentence.

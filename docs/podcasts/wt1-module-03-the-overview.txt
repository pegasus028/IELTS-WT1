QUILLMOOR OBSERVATORY — MODULE 03 PODCAST BRIEFING
IELTS ACADEMIC WRITING TASK 1 · MODULE 03: THE OVERVIEW
Listeners: Thai high school students, CEFR A2 to B2, aiming for Writing Band 7 to 8
Topic: what an overview is and why it gates Task Achievement, the three ways to spoil
one, and how to write it every time

================================================================
HOW TO PRESENT THIS
================================================================
Two people talking the topic through with each other. The whole episode must be
UNDER SIX MINUTES. No welcome, no introductions, no "today we are going to talk
about", no small talk: the first sentence is the first fact below. Speak slowly and
clearly, in short sentences and everyday CEFR B1 words. Explain every IELTS term in
plain words the first time it is used. Say each example sentence slowly enough to be
written down. Use only the examples given here. Never mention notes, a document, a
source, a file, an app or where the information came from. Never speak to a
listener: no "you", no audience, no goodbye. Cover every point below, in order; if
time is short, shorten the chat around a point, never drop the point. Stop after the
last idea. Say numbers slowly and in full (twenty-five percent, not 25 percent), and
when an example describes a chart, describe the chart in words first.

================================================================
1. WHAT AN OVERVIEW IS (about 1 minute 30)
================================================================

The overview is the second paragraph. It begins "Overall," and gives two or three
general truths about the whole chart: what rose, what fell, what was largest, what
stayed the same. It is about twenty to seventy words. If the chart were a news story,
the overview would be the headline, not the article.

No paragraph is worth more. The Task Achievement descriptors climb like this. Band 5:
"There may be a tendency to focus on details (without referring to the bigger
picture)." Band 6: "A relevant overview is attempted." Band 7: "It presents a clear
overview" and "main trends or differences are identified." So a report with no
overview cannot score above Band 5 for Task Achievement, however good the details
are. That is a ceiling, a roof on the score.

Take a line graph of visitors to three museums in a European city from 2010 to 2020.
The Science Museum line climbs all the way. The Art Gallery and the History Museum
both slope down, and the History Museum is lowest every year. The overview: "Overall,
the Science Museum grew strongly over the decade, whereas the other two museums lost
visitors. The History Museum remained the least visited throughout."

There is a simple test. Cover the numbers on the chart. If the paragraph still tells a
true and useful story, it is an overview. If it needs the numbers to mean anything, it
is a detail paragraph in disguise.

================================================================
2. THREE WAYS TO SPOIL AN OVERVIEW (about 2 minutes)
================================================================

One: vague. A bar chart shows the weekly hours that four age groups spent on social
media, streaming video and gaming in 2023. "Overall, there were some differences
between the age groups." That is true of every chart ever drawn, so it says nothing.
A line graph shows internet access in four countries going up. "Overall, internet
access changed" hides the direction. The word is "rose". A vague overview keeps Task
Achievement at Band 6 or below, because Band 7 needs the main trends identified.

Two: data values. "Overall, teenagers spent twenty-one hours a week on social media"
is a detail sentence that starts with "Overall". The number belongs in paragraph 3.
But not every number is forbidden. Years are fine: "Overall, arrivals rose to a peak
in 2019, collapsed in 2020 and 2021, and then recovered strongly." Category names
with numbers inside them are fine: "the thirteen to seventeen age group". Counting the
categories is fine: "all three museums". A multiple in words is fine: "almost
doubled". What is not fine is any value from the data, even a rounded one. "Overall,
around eighty percent of energy came from fossil fuels" is still a data value.

Three: opinion. A pie chart shows how a secondary school student spent her weekly
money: food and drinks were the biggest slice, savings the smallest. "Overall, the
student should have saved more money" is a judgement, not a fact on the chart. An
opinion inside the overview keeps Task Achievement at Band 5 or below. The correct
version: "Overall, food and drinks made up the largest share of spending, and savings
the smallest."

And a wrong overview is worse than a vague one. On a line graph of internet access,
Thailand rises fast but stays below Japan in 2025. "Overall, Thailand overtook Japan"
is a false main trend, and it limits Task Achievement to Band 6 or below.

================================================================
3. WRITING IT EVERY TIME (about 1 minute 30)
================================================================

First, find the headline. Look at the whole chart and ask: what would a newspaper
say in a few words? For the museums: Science Museum up, others down. For two pie
charts of electricity sources in one country, in 2000 and 2020: coal loses its lead.

Then use the overview pattern: Overall, [one general claim], while or whereas
[a contrasting general claim]. The first claim is the biggest movement or
difference. The second is the contrast or the exception. A second sentence can add
one more truth: what was largest all the time, what stayed the same, or that the
later years are only forecasts.

On the pie charts, coal falls from almost half of all electricity to a much smaller
share, natural gas becomes the biggest slice, wind and solar grow from a tiny slice to
one of the largest, and nuclear and hydro hardly move. The overview: "Overall, coal
lost more than half of its share, while natural gas and wind and solar grew to become
the leading sources. Nuclear and hydro barely changed."

On a table of teachers' pay and weekly hours in five countries: "Overall, German
teachers were the best paid and worked the shortest week, whereas Thai teachers
earned far less than the rest."

Whereas, while, in contrast, both: one of these words makes the overview compare,
which the task sentence asks for.

================================================================
THE THING WORTH REMEMBERING
================================================================

Paragraph 2, "Overall," two or three general truths, one comparison, and no data
values. Without it, Task Achievement stops at Band 5; with a clear one, Band 7 is
possible.

QUILLMOOR OBSERVATORY — MODULE 09 PODCAST BRIEFING
IELTS ACADEMIC WRITING TASK 1 · MODULE 09: SPACE MODE
Listeners: Thai high school students, CEFR A2 to B2, aiming for Writing Band 7 to 8
Topic: maps: compass points and position, change verbs in the passive with the right
tense, and the overview of a map

================================================================
HOW TO PRESENT THIS
================================================================
Two people talking the topic through with each other. The whole episode must be
UNDER SIX MINUTES. No welcome, no introductions, no "today we are going to talk
about", no small talk: the first sentence is the first fact below. Speak slowly and
clearly, in short sentences and everyday CEFR B1 words. Explain every IELTS term in
plain words the first time it is used. Say each example sentence slowly enough to be
written down. Use only the examples given here. Never mention notes, a document, a
source, a file, an app or where the information came from. Never speak to a
listener: no "you", no audience, no goodbye. Cover every point below, in order; if
time is short, shorten the chat around a point, never drop the point. Stop after the
last idea. Say numbers slowly and in full (twenty-five percent, not 25 percent), and
when an example describes a chart, describe the chart in words first.

================================================================
1. COMPASS AND POSITION (about 1 minute 30)
================================================================

SPACE mode is for maps, usually the same place at two dates. The example is two maps
of the centre of a town called Riverside, in 1995 and in 2025.

Here is the 1995 map. A river runs along the top, the northern edge, and a bridge
carries the main road across it. The main road runs north to south through the
middle. In 1995, west of the road, there was a car park near the river, a bus station
to the south of it, and a park in the south-west corner. Just west of the road stood
a cinema and some small shops. The whole eastern side was a factory.

The examiner reads a map with north at the top, so position is given with the
compass: in the north, to the east of the main road, in the south-west corner, along
the northern edge. "Left", "right", "above" and "below" describe the paper, not the
town. The error: "The park is on the left at the bottom of the map." The fix: "In
1995, the park was in the south-west corner."

Position phrases fix one feature against another: "next to"; "adjacent to", which
means right next to; "opposite", which means facing it across a road or a river;
"along"; "between". The verbs are simple: was located, was situated, stood. Rivers
and roads "run". For example: "In 1995, the factory was located to the east of the
main road, opposite the cinema."

================================================================
2. CHANGE VERBS AND TENSES (about 2 minutes)
================================================================

Now the 2025 map. By 2025, the car park was a shopping mall. The cinema and the small
shops were gone, and a pedestrian square with cafes stood in their place. Apartment
blocks stood where the factory had been. The main road south of the bridge was closed
to cars, and a new ring road ran along the southern edge. The park was bigger,
stretching further east. The river, the bridge and the bus station were unchanged.

A map never shows who made a change, so the natural voice is passive. The error: "The
council demolished the cinema." Nothing on the map shows a council. The change verbs
are a small kit: built, added, demolished, knocked down, converted into, replaced by,
extended, relocated, pedestrianised. "Extended" means it grew where it was.
"Relocated" means it moved to a new site. "Pedestrianised" means given to people on
foot.

Two shapes say old and new in one sentence. First: "The factory in the east was
demolished and replaced by apartment blocks." Second, from the other side: "The
cinema and the small shops were knocked down to make way for a pedestrian square."

The tense comes from the titles of the two maps. Two past dates, like 1995 and 2025
on the Riverside maps: past simple passive, "was converted". A past map and a map of
today: present perfect passive for the change, "has been converted", and present
simple for what is there now, "a mall now occupies the site". A map of today and a
plan for the future: future passive. One more example: a university campus today and
a plan for it. "A new library will be built in the south of the campus, on part of
the sports field." Writing "has been built" about a plan says it already happened.
That is inaccurate.

================================================================
3. THE OVERVIEW OF A MAP (about 1 minute 30)
================================================================

A map report needs an overview, the "Overall" paragraph, just as much as a line graph
does. Without it, Task Achievement cannot go above Band 5.

The map overview answers three questions. What kind of place did it become: more
residential, meaning more homes, or more commercial, more green, more built-up? What
was removed and added? What stayed the same? Two or three sentences, starting with
"Overall", with no measurements.

For Riverside: "Overall, the town centre changed from industrial and commercial use
to housing and leisure, and traffic was moved out of the centre, although the river,
the bridge and the bus station remained." What stayed the same is a key feature too.

Two weak overviews. Too vague: "Overall, there were many changes to the town between
1995 and 2025." With a vague overview, Task Achievement cannot go above Band 6.
Invented: "The factory closed because people wanted cleaner air." A map shows what
changed, never why. A reason is invented material.

Then group the changes for paragraphs three and four, either by area, such as the
east and then the west, or by type of change, such as buildings first and then roads
and green space. Never describe the whole 1995 map and then the whole 2025 map: that
leaves the comparing to the reader.

================================================================
THE THING WORTH REMEMBERING
================================================================

Compass points, never left and right. Passive change verbs, with the tense taken from
the two dates. An overview that names the kind of change and what stayed the same.

# The Reading Room, Task 1: Harry Potter scenes for the Observatory (teacher only)

**Who this is for:** T.Chris, for class M4.1 (EN31208, 30 students, B1/B2) and the six Tutoring tutees (Aeh, JingJing, Pear, Mikii, Donut, Soda).
**IP level: C.** The app (Quillmoor Academy, the Observatory) is an original world and never names Harry Potter, Hogwarts, the houses, spells or characters. The real scenes live only on this page, in class discussion and in what you say aloud. Nothing here goes into the app, the GitHub Pages site or a public slide.

**How to use this page.** Each app chapter has one canon scene that contains data a chartwright could describe: points, scores, counts, a recipe, a map. The scene is the warm-up and the talking point. The Task 1 visual you set is always **original and real-world** (the data tables below are invented for teaching [not from sources]); never ask students to write a report on the wizarding data itself. Book numbers and chapter titles are the UK Bloomsbury editions; the Thai Nanmeebooks editions keep the same chapter order. Each scene says **book** or **film**. Summaries are in my own words; at most one short line is quoted. Chapter numbers or figures marked **[check]** came from memory and must be checked against a copy before you quote them.

**Why Harry Potter stays outside the app.** The site is public. Rowling's names and Warner Bros.' marks are enforced when used as branding or copied at length; a short scene discussed in class is ordinary teaching use. Discuss, quote one line, never paste chapters into a shared doc.

**One rule for every task below.** 20 minutes, at least 150 words (aim for 160–190), four paragraphs (introduction, overview, Detail A, Detail B), no conclusion, no opinion, no reasons. In the real test one examiner marks the report on four criteria (TA, CC, LR, GRA), and Task 2 counts twice as much as Task 1.

## 1. App chapter 1 — The Letter · triage (mode, units, time frame)

**Scene (book):** *Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone*, chapter 3, "The Letters from No One".
One letter arrives for Harry and Uncle Vernon takes it. More letters follow, and the number keeps growing: twelve on the Friday, twenty-four hidden inside eggs on the Saturday, and on the Sunday (no post, Vernon thinks) thirty or forty letters shoot out of the fireplace. The family flees to a hut on a rock. [check: which days the first one or two letters came]
**Film note:** the film turns the Sunday into a flood of hundreds of letters; for numbers, use the book.

**Why it fits:** the letters per day are a perfect TREND: a time axis, one unit (letters), and a sharp rise. "Thirty or forty" is a lesson in approximation: the report says *around 35*, never an exact figure the source does not give.

**Discussion:**
1. If you drew the letters as a graph, what would go on the horizontal axis, and what is the unit on the vertical axis? So which mode is it?
2. The book says "thirty or forty". How would you report that number honestly in one sentence?

**Original Task 1 visual (TREND, line graph):** *The graph below shows the number of food-delivery orders per day from one restaurant in Bangkok during one week in March 2025.*

| Day | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Orders | 120 | 135 | 150 | 210 | 380 | 520 | 460 |

Mode TREND (past dates → past simple). Grouping: Monday–Thursday (slow, steady rise) vs Friday–Sunday (sharp rise, peak on Saturday, small fall on Sunday). Key features: overall rise; weekend far higher; peak Saturday.

**2-minute warm-up — "Thirty seconds of triage":** flash three visuals for ten seconds each (this line graph, a pie chart, a process diagram). Students call out the mode, the units and the time frame. Nobody reads a number.

## 2. App chapter 2 — The Four-Part Charm · paragraphs and paraphrase

**Scene (book):** *Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone*, chapter 5, "Diagon Alley".
Hagrid takes Harry to the wizards' bank and explains the money in one breath: gold Galleons, silver Sickles, bronze Knuts, "seventeen silver Sickles to a Galleon and twenty-nine Knuts to a Sickle". Then they shop from the school list: robes, books, a cauldron, scales, a wand.
**Film note:** the film shows the bank and the shops but does not give the exchange rate aloud [check]; use the book.

**Why it fits:** Hagrid's sentence is a one-line introduction to a table. Students paraphrase it, then sort the shopping list into two groups: the skeleton of a four-part report.

**Discussion:**
1. Rewrite Hagrid's money rule as the first sentence of a report: "The table shows…". Do not copy his words.
2. Harry's list has clothes, books and equipment. If you had to write only two detail paragraphs, which two groups would you make?

**Original Task 1 visual (TREND + COMPARE, table):** *The table below shows the average cost, in baht, of four items of school equipment in Bangkok in 2016, 2020 and 2024.*

| Item | 2016 | 2020 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Uniform (one set) | 650 | 750 | 900 |
| Textbooks (one year) | 1,800 | 2,100 | 2,600 |
| School bag | 450 | 480 | 500 |
| Stationery (one year) | 300 | 310 | 320 |

Years in columns → TREND. Grouping: big rises (uniform, textbooks) vs small rises (bag, stationery). Key features: all four rose; textbooks most expensive throughout and rose most.

**2-minute warm-up — "Paraphrase, don't copy":** write the task sentence on the board. Pairs have 60 seconds to change three things: the verb (shows → illustrates), the noun phrase (the average cost → how much … cost on average), the time phrase (in 2016, 2020 and 2024 → over an eight-year period). Read two out; the class finds any run of eight copied words.

## 3. App chapter 3 — The Watchtower · overview and claim + number

**Scene (book):** *Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone*, chapter 17, "The Man with Two Faces".
At the end-of-year feast the House Cup totals are read: Gryffindor fourth with 312, Hufflepuff 352, Ravenclaw 426, Slytherin first with 472. Then last-minute points are given to Ron (50), Hermione (50), Harry (60) and Neville (10). Gryffindor's 170 extra points take it to 482, ten ahead of Slytherin.
*One line:* "There are all kinds of courage." (Dumbledore, before Neville's ten points)
**Film note:** the film keeps the order of the awards but not every total; use the book.

**Why it fits:** two bar charts in one scene: before and after. The overview is the gate: "Slytherin led, but Gryffindor rose from last place to first" says everything without a single figure; the details prove it with 312, 482 and 472.

**Discussion:**
1. Say the overview of the first totals in one sentence with no numbers.
2. Now give two detail sentences, each with a claim and a number. Where is the comparison?

**Original Task 1 visual (COMPARE, bar chart, two years):** *The bar chart below shows the number of books borrowed from a school library by students in four year groups in the first and second terms of 2025.*

| Year group | Term 1 | Term 2 |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | 310 | 520 |
| M2 | 360 | 480 |
| M3 | 430 | 410 |
| M4 | 470 | 400 |

Grouping: younger years (M1, M2) borrowed more in term 2 vs older years (M3, M4) borrowed slightly fewer. Key features: M1 rose from lowest to highest (a crossover in rank); M4 fell from highest to lowest.

**2-minute warm-up — "Overview or not?":** read six sentences about the House Cup aloud. Students hold up O (overview) or D (detail). A sentence with any value (even "about 480") is never an overview.

## 4. App chapter 4 — The Tide Clock · grouping and TREND language

**Scene (book):** *Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone*, chapter 15, "The Forbidden Forest".
After the dragon is smuggled out at midnight, Harry and Hermione are caught out of bed, and so is Neville, who came to warn them. Professor McGonagall takes fifty points from each: 150 in one night. Gryffindor falls from first place to last, and the whole house turns against them. [check: the points taken from Slytherin for Malfoy the same night]
**Film note:** the film shows the detention in the Forest but the points loss is much less prominent; use the book.

**Why it fits:** a line for Gryffindor's points would show a steady rise through the year, then a sharp fall in one night: the language of degree (sharply, steadily) and of peaks and troughs.

**Discussion:**
1. Describe Gryffindor's points across the year with two trend verbs and two degree words.
2. Hufflepuff and Ravenclaw "moved up" the table that night without gaining a point. How would you report that accurately?

**Original Task 1 visual (TREND, line graph, four lines):** *The graph below shows the number of visitors, in thousands, to four attractions in Chiang Mai from January to June 2025.*

| Month | Jan | Feb | Mar | Apr | May | Jun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Night Bazaar (indoor) | 40 | 42 | 45 | 50 | 54 | 58 |
| Art Centre (indoor) | 15 | 16 | 18 | 21 | 24 | 26 |
| Doi Suthep trail (outdoor) | 60 | 55 | 41 | 30 | 22 | 18 |
| River park (outdoor) | 35 | 33 | 30 | 24 | 20 | 17 |

Grouping: indoor attractions rose steadily vs outdoor attractions fell (the trail most sharply, by 42 thousand). Key features: a crossover between February and March, when the Night Bazaar overtook the trail; the trail fell from the highest figure (60) to 18 in June, just above the river park, which finished lowest at 17.

**2-minute warm-up — "Group them!":** give pairs four cards, one per line. They have 60 seconds to sort them into two piles and name each pile in three words ("indoor, rising steadily").

## 5. App chapter 5 — The Hall of Scales · COMPARE (no trend verbs)

**Scene (book):** *Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire*, chapter 20, "The First Task".
Each champion faces a dragon and the five judges raise their wands to show marks out of ten. Harry gets 40 in total and is level in first place with Krum. Karkaroff, the Durmstrang headmaster, gives Harry only four, and Ron is furious. [check: each judge's mark for Harry — about 8, 9, 9, 10 and 4]
**Film note:** the film skips the judges' marks; use the book.

**Why it fits:** five judges, one task, one moment: no time axis, so nothing "rose". Students compare: *higher than*, *twice as high as*, *the lowest mark, given by …*. Karkaroff's mark is the exception that belongs in the overview.

**Discussion:**
1. Compare Karkaroff's mark with the highest mark using "… times" or "less than half".
2. Why does one very low mark matter for a report? Where does it go: overview or detail?

**Original Task 1 visual (COMPARE, table):** *The table below shows the marks, out of ten, that five judges gave to three singers in a school singing contest in 2025.*

| Judge | A | B | C | D | E | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Singer 1 | 8 | 9 | 9 | 10 | 4 | 40 |
| Singer 2 | 7 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 9 | 40 |
| Singer 3 | 6 | 6 | 7 | 7 | 6 | 32 |

Grouping: the two leaders (tied on 40) vs Singer 3. Key features: tie at the top; Judge E's mark for Singer 1 is the exception (4, less than half of Judge D's 10); Singer 3 lowest from every judge except E, where Singer 3 (6) beat Singer 1 (4).

**2-minute warm-up — "No trend verbs":** read five sentences about the table. Any sentence with *rose, fell, increased* gets a buzzer. Students fix it with a comparison.

## 6. App chapter 6 (part 1) — The Cellars · SEQUENCE (process, passive)

**Scene (book):** *Harry Potter and the Half-Blood Prince*, chapter 9, "The Half-Blood Prince".
In Professor Slughorn's first lesson the class brews the Draught of Living Death for a prize of Felix Felicis. Harry's second-hand textbook has handwritten notes that change the recipe: crush the sopophorous bean with the flat of a silver dagger instead of cutting it, and add a clockwise stir after every seventh anticlockwise stir. Harry follows the notes and wins. [check: chapter number]
**Film note:** the same lesson appears in the film, with Harry winning the small bottle of luck.

**Why it fits:** a recipe is a linear process. The report uses the present simple passive (*the bean is crushed*), a sequencer for every stage, and an overview that counts the stages and names the start and the end. Hermione follows the printed book; Harry follows a better frame. That is the spellbook idea in one scene.

**Discussion:**
1. Put two steps of the recipe into the present simple passive with a sequencer.
2. Harry's notes improve a printed recipe. How is a personal template like those notes, and how is it different from copying?

**Original Task 1 visual (SEQUENCE, process diagram, 7 stages):** *The diagram below shows how dried mango is produced.*

| Stage | Picture label |
|---|---|
| 1 | ripe mangoes picked by hand |
| 2 | washed and peeled |
| 3 | cut into thin slices |
| 4 | soaked in sugar syrup for 6 hours |
| 5 | dried in a solar dryer for 12 hours |
| 6 | cooled and checked |
| 7 | packed into bags and sent to shops |

Linear, seven stages, start = ripe mangoes, end = packed dried mango. Grouping: preparation (1–4) vs drying and distribution (5–7).

**2-minute warm-up — "Passive chain":** each student in turn says one stage in the passive with a new sequencer ("First, …", "Next, …", "After this, …", "Once …, …", "Finally, …"). Repeat a sequencer and you sit down.

## 7. App chapter 6 (part 2) — The Moor Maps · SPACE (compass points, change verbs)

**Scene (book):** *Harry Potter and the Prisoner of Azkaban*, chapter 10, "The Marauder's Map".
Fred and George give Harry an old blank parchment that becomes a map of the whole school, with every person shown as a moving, labelled dot. They point out seven secret passages out of the castle: Filch knows four, one has caved in, one has the Whomping Willow planted over its entrance, and one leads into the cellar of the sweet shop in Hogsmeade. [check: the passage counts]
**Film note:** the film shows the map and the Honeydukes passage but not the full list of passages; use the book.

**Why it fits:** students describe places on a map with compass points, not left and right, and say what is blocked, built over or still open: the change verbs of a map task.

**Discussion:**
1. Describe where one secret passage starts and where it ends, using a compass point and a landmark.
2. One passage "caved in" and one has a tree "planted over the entrance". Turn both into passive change sentences.

**Original Task 1 visual (SPACE, two maps):** *The maps below show the riverside area of a village in Ayutthaya in 2005 and 2025.*

| Feature | 2005 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Wooden market (north-west) | market | demolished → car park |
| Rice field (east) | fields | converted into a housing estate |
| Temple (centre) | temple | unchanged |
| School (south) | small school | extended to the west |
| River path (south-east) | footpath | replaced by a road |
| — | — | new bridge built across the river in the north-east |

Overview: the area became more built-up and modern; the temple remained unchanged. Grouping: removed/replaced features vs new and extended features.

**2-minute warm-up — "Not left, not right":** one student describes where a classroom object is using only compass points and landmarks ("to the north of the door"). The others point to it. Saying *left* or *right* costs a point.

## 8. App chapter 7 — The Library and the Newsroom · two visuals, accuracy, cohesion

**Scene (book):** *Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire*, chapter 18, "The Weighing of the Wands". [check: chapter number]
The reporter Rita Skeeter interviews Harry in a cupboard. Her acid-green Quick-Quotes Quill writes a story Harry never told: tears in his eyes, a dramatic past. The article that follows is mostly invented.
**Film note:** the film keeps the self-writing quill and the cupboard interview.

**Why it fits:** the Gossip Quill in the app is a cousin of this quill: it writes figures nobody measured. A report is the opposite: every sentence can be checked against the visual. With two visuals, the rule is strict: one overview with a headline for each, one paragraph each, and never combine their figures.

**Discussion:**
1. Rita's quill adds details nobody said. What would "adding details" look like in a chart report? Give one example of an invented figure and one of an opinion.
2. How could Harry check her article? How does a reader check your report?

**Original Task 1 visual (two visuals: pie + bar):** *The pie chart shows where teenagers in Thailand got most of their news in 2024, and the bar chart shows the percentage who trusted each source.*

| Source | Main source of news (% of teenagers) | Trust the source (% of teenagers) |
|---|---|---|
| Social media | 52 | 31 |
| TV news | 21 | 64 |
| News websites | 15 | 58 |
| Friends and family | 9 | 47 |
| Newspapers | 3 | 70 |

The two visuals measure different things: never add or combine the percentages. Overview: social media was by far the main source, yet it was the least trusted; newspapers were the least used but the most trusted.

**2-minute warm-up — "Spot the fake figure":** read a six-sentence report on the table with two invented figures and one reason ("because teenagers are lazy"). Teams race to find all three.

## 9. App chapter 8 — The Almanac Examination · selection and key features

**Scene (book):** *Harry Potter and the Half-Blood Prince*, chapter 5, "An Excess of Phlegm".
The O.W.L. results arrive by owl at the Burrow. Harry's sheet: Astronomy A, Care of Magical Creatures E, Charms E, Defence Against the Dark Arts O, Divination P, Herbology E, History of Magic D, Potions E, Transfiguration E. Hermione has ten O.W.L.s, nine of them Outstanding; Ron has seven. [check: the full grade list and Hermione's one E]
**Film note:** the results scene is not in the film.

**Why it fits:** nine subjects, three students: too much data to list. A strong report selects the key features: Harry's one O, his two fails, Hermione's near-perfect sheet. Selection is the gate from Band 7 to Band 8.

**Discussion:**
1. Choose the three most important facts about Harry's results. What do you leave out on purpose?
2. Write one sentence that compares Harry's and Hermione's results with a number.

**Original Task 1 visual (COMPARE across two years, table):** *The table below shows the percentage of students at one school who achieved an A grade in five subjects in 2022 and 2024.*

| Subject | 2022 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| English | 28 | 41 |
| Mathematics | 22 | 33 |
| Science | 25 | 33 |
| Social Studies | 30 | 27 |
| Art | 45 | 38 |

Grouping: three subjects rose (English most, by 13 percentage points) vs two fell (Art and Social Studies). Key features: Art highest in 2022 but overtaken by English in 2024; the rises were larger than the falls.

**2-minute warm-up — "Choose four":** show the table for 30 seconds, then hide it. Students write the four key features they remember. Compare: the features most people remembered are the ones the overview needs.

## 10. Midwinter Tournament (optional, midterm week) — timed report

**Scene (book):** *Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone*, chapter 11, "Quidditch".
Harry's first match, against Slytherin. His broom is jinxed and nearly throws him off; then he catches the Snitch in his mouth. Gryffindor wins 170 to 60. [check: the final score]
**Film note:** the film keeps the jinxed broom and the Snitch caught in the mouth.

**Why it fits:** the score is data with two parts: goals (10 points each) and the Snitch (150). 170 = 150 + two goals; 60 = six goals. A COMPARE task under a clock: how much of the winning score came from one catch?

**Discussion:**
1. What fraction of Gryffindor's score came from the Snitch? Say it with "just under nine-tenths" or "the vast majority".
2. If you have twenty minutes, what do you write first, and when do you stop to check?

**Original Task 1 visual (COMPARE, two pie charts), timed at 20 minutes:** *The pie charts below show how the points were scored by two school basketball teams over the 2025 season.*

| Type of score | Team A (total 1,000 points) | Team B (total 800 points) |
|---|---|---|
| Three-point shots | 45% | 20% |
| Two-point shots | 40% | 60% |
| Free throws | 15% | 20% |

Two pies with different totals: compare shares, and do not compare percentages as if they were points. Overview: Team A relied most on three-point shots, Team B on two-point shots; free throws were the smallest share for both.

**2-minute warm-up — "Beat the clock":** students say the 20-minute protocol from memory in order: 30 seconds triage, plan to minute 4, write to minute 17, check the Ten Wards to minute 20. Wrong order, start again.
